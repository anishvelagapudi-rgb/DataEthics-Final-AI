Ethics & Privacy

Built Responsibly

An app designed to fight manipulation must be held to a higher standard of transparency. Here's how we think about the hard questions.

Ethical Challenges

The hard questions we ask ourselves

⚖️ Who decides what counts as "misleading"?

Defining misrepresentation is itself a subjective act. [APP NAME] mitigates this by focusing on structural manipulation patterns — e.g., a bar chart with a truncated y-axis — rather than making political or factual truth judgments. Our taxonomy of manipulation types is publicly documented and open to community feedback, and we plan to work with media literacy researchers to refine it over time.

🤖 Algorithmic Bias

AI models reflect the data they are trained on. If our training data overrepresents certain media outlets, political viewpoints, or languages, the model will be systematically biased in its flags. We address this through diverse training corpora, regular bias audits, and making our flagging methodology transparent. Users can always see why content was flagged — not just that it was.

🗣️ Chilling Effects on Speech

A tool that flags content as misleading could discourage creators from publishing nuanced or unconventional analysis — even when that analysis is legitimate. We are careful to frame flags as "potential concerns" rather than verdicts, and we always include an explanation. Publishers whose content is flagged can submit a formal review request.

🔁 Creating New Misinformation

If users blindly trust [APP NAME]'s outputs, we risk replacing one form of misinformation dependency with another. Our Explanation Mode is designed to build the user's own critical thinking skills — not to be an oracle. We prominently display confidence levels and encourage independent verification.

📡 Surveillance Risk

Scanning content you browse creates a detailed picture of your media consumption. [APP NAME] uses on-device processing wherever possible and never stores browsing history on our servers without explicit consent. We will never sell data to advertisers, data brokers, or government agencies.

Mitigation

How we address these concerns

📖

Open Methodology

Our taxonomy of manipulation patterns is publicly available. Anyone can read, critique, and propose changes to how we define misrepresentation.

🧪

Regular Bias Audits

Third-party researchers will conduct periodic audits of the model's outputs across political spectrum, geography, and language.

🙋

Human Review Process

Any content creator or publisher can appeal a flag. Human reviewers — not just the AI — evaluate disputes.

🏠

On-Device Processing

Analysis runs locally on your device when possible, minimizing how much content ever reaches our servers.

Privacy Policy

Your data, your control

What We Collect

When you use [APP NAME], we may collect: (1) anonymized content snippets submitted for analysis, (2) aggregate usage statistics (e.g., how many articles scanned per session), and (3) account information if you create a premium account (email, subscription status). We do not collect full browsing history, personal identifiers linked to specific articles, or any data from content you view but do not submit for analysis.

How We Use It

Anonymized analysis data is used solely to improve model accuracy. Aggregate usage statistics help us understand which features are most useful. We will never sell, license, or share your data with third parties for advertising or profiling purposes.

Your Rights

You may request deletion of any account data at any time. On-device processing means most analysis never leaves your device. You can opt out of anonymous data sharing in settings at any time without losing access to core features.

Data Retention

Anonymized analysis logs are retained for a maximum of 90 days to support model improvement, then permanently deleted. Account information is retained until you request deletion. We do not retain identifiable browsing data under any circumstances.
