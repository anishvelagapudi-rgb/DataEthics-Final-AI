Ethics & Privacy

Responsibility Comes First

We built TruthLens to defend informed decision-making, not to decide what people should think. Here's how we navigate that line.

Ethical Challenges

What We Had to Get Right

Algorithmic Bias

Any AI system trained on existing data can inherit political or cultural biases. If TruthLens flagged left-leaning content differently than right-leaning content, or vice versa, it would become a tool of partisan manipulation rather than a check on it. We address this by focusing detection on structural manipulation techniques (misleading chart scales, decontextualized statistics, emotionally loaded framing) that apply regardless of political viewpoint, and by publishing our methodology so outside researchers can audit it.

Risk of Censorship

There is a meaningful difference between labeling content as potentially misleading and restricting it. Policymakers could push for tools like TruthLens to suppress or demote content rather than just annotate it, and that would be a misuse. According to the brief we produced for this project, "Governments and technology companies would need to create guidelines to ensure these systems are not only fair and accurate but also not used to censor opinions." TruthLens will never remove, block, or demote content; it only adds context.

False Positives & Over-Flagging

Incorrectly labeling accurate, well-sourced content as misinformation damages user trust, harms publishers, and, most importantly, undermines the product's core purpose. An over-sensitive system teaches users to distrust the tool rather than to distrust manipulation. Our approach prioritizes explaining reasoning: if TruthLens flags something, it must be able to articulate exactly why, so the user can make an informed judgment rather than just deferring to a label.

Privacy of Browsing Behavior

Scanning what users read in real time raises serious privacy concerns. A log of which articles someone reads can reveal political views, health concerns, religious beliefs, and other deeply personal information. TruthLens is designed with a privacy-first architecture: content is analyzed locally on the device where possible, and we do not build a history of individual browsing activity. What you read is not stored, transmitted, or sold.

Transparency & Accountability

A "black box" system that renders verdicts without explanation is unacceptable for a tool that influences what people trust. Every flag TruthLens generates must include a plain-language explanation of the specific tactic detected. Users must be able to contest flags. Patterns of contested flags must trigger internal audits. We hold ourselves to the same transparency standards we ask of the media we analyze.

Mitigation

How We Address These Concerns

Open Methodology

Detection criteria, training data sources, and flagging logic are published publicly. External researchers and journalists can audit our methods at any time.

No Content Removal

TruthLens adds context; it never removes, demotes, or blocks content. The user always retains full control over what they read and share.

Human Review Process

Users can contest any flag through a human review queue. Patterns of contested flags automatically trigger a methodology audit to catch systemic errors.

Privacy-First Architecture

Content is analyzed locally on-device where possible. We do not store or transmit the full text of articles you read, and we do not build individual browsing profiles.

Responsible AI Use

How We Used AI to Build This

Claude Code (Anthropic)

This website was built with the assistance of Claude Code, Anthropic's AI coding tool. Claude Code was used to generate the HTML/CSS structure and fill in page content based on our written research brief, elevator pitch, and presentation materials. All source content (the research brief, findings, policy implications, and ethical analysis) was written by our team. Claude Code served as a development tool, not an author. Logs of our Claude Code sessions are included in the project's Google Drive folder for transparency.

Privacy Policy

Your Data & Your Rights

What We Collect

TruthLens collects anonymized usage statistics (such as which features you use and how often flags are contested) and basic device and browser type for compatibility purposes. We do not store the full text of articles you read, we do not record your browsing history, and we do not collect personally identifiable information unless you create an account.

How We Use It

Usage data is used exclusively to improve detection accuracy and product reliability. It is never sold to third parties, never used for advertising targeting, and never shared with government agencies absent a lawful order. Aggregate, anonymized statistics may be published in transparency reports.

Your Rights

You may request complete deletion of your account and all associated data at any time from the settings page. You can opt out of all analytics while still using TruthLens's core detection features; analytics are optional, not required. If you are in the EU, you have additional rights under GDPR including data portability and the right to restrict processing.

Data Retention

Anonymized aggregate statistics are retained for up to 24 months to support longitudinal accuracy improvements. No individually identifiable browsing history is ever retained. Account data is deleted within 30 days of an account deletion request.
